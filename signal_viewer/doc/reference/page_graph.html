Graph page

The editing page. It shows the active section and is where sections are created, the signal is processed and peaks are detected and corrected. The Sections and Parameter Inputs docks appear with it. How-to: Working with sections, Detecting and editing peaks.

The two plots

ElementContent
Signal plot (top)The processed signal of the active section (blue line) and its peaks (yellow dots; red cross when hovered). While a section is being created, the grey region selector. With Section Overview on, the sections of the base section as coloured regions.
Rate plot (bottom)The rate in beats per minute computed from the current peaks with the Rate Calculation settings. Empty until a section has peaks. Shares the time axis with the signal plot.
Time axis (top edge)Elapsed time since the section start as days, HH:MM, HH:MM:SS or seconds, depending on the zoom level.
LegendNames the curves: Signal, Peaks, Rate.

Mouse and keyboard

InputEffect
Mouse wheelZoom the time axis around the cursor.
Left-drag on empty plotPan along the time axis. The y-axis follows the visible data.
Right-dragZoom the time axis around the point where the drag started.
Click on the signal curveAdd a peak at the nearest extreme within the click radius (unlocked user sections only).
Click on a peak dotRemove that peak.
Ctrl+left-drag, middle-dragDraw a selection rectangle.
Ctrl+FDetect peaks inside the rectangle with the selected method and add them.
Ctrl+DRemove every peak inside the rectangle.
Click on a section regionMake that section active (Section Overview on, base section active).
Drag the region selectorMove the whole range or one of its edges while creating a section.

The click tolerances are settings: Line click width (how far from the curve a click still counts) and Click radius (how far around the click the extreme is searched). See Settings.

Channel switcher

The Channel drop-down in the main toolbar lists every numeric column of the file. Selecting one shows that channel with its own sections; the first switch reads the channel from the file. Sections of the other channels are kept. See Channels.
